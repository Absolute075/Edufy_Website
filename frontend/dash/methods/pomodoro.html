🍅 Pomodoro Technique

Short, focused sprints with restorative breaks

How the Pomodoro Technique Was Born
The Pomodoro Technique was developed in the late 1980s by Francesco Cirillo, an Italian university student.
Origin

Year: around 1987
Place: Italy

Story:
While studying at university, Cirillo struggled to maintain focus. He experimented with a kitchen timer to improve his concentration. The timer he used was shaped like a red tomato, which is why he named his method the Pomodoro Technique (pomodoro means “tomato” in Italian).

How It Works
  • Set a timer for 25 minutes — this is one “pomodoro”.
  • Work fully focused, without distractions.
  • Take a short break (5 minutes) after the timer rings.
  • After 4 “pomodoros,” take a long break (15–30 minutes).
Idea
The method is based on the principle that the brain works best in short, focused sessions, and frequent breaks help prevent burnout.
Publication
Francesco Cirillo later described his method in the book: Francesco Cirillo — The Pomodoro Technique (2006). Today, it is one of the most popular time‑management and productivity techniques worldwide.

What is the Feynman Technique?

Explain simply to understand deeply

📗 Definition
The Feynman Technique is a learning method named after physicist Richard Feynman. The core idea is to explain complex concepts in simple words, as if teaching someone who knows nothing about the subject.
💡 Main Idea
“If you cannot explain something simply, you don’t fully understand it.” By forcing yourself to teach it, you reveal gaps and solidify understanding.

Step-by-Step Process

A repeatable loop to master any topic

1) Choose a topic
Pick a concept you want to learn. Write its name at the top of a page.
2) Explain simply
Explain it aloud or on paper as if teaching a beginner. Avoid jargon and filler like “obviously” or “you know”.
3) Identify gaps
Where your explanation gets vague or complex — that’s a knowledge gap. Mark it clearly.
4) Study and refine
Return to the sources, fill the gaps, then repeat the explanation more clearly.
5) (Optional) Use analogies
Create simple analogies to clarify abstract ideas. Short, concrete comparisons help memory.

Example

Turning complexity into clarity

Topic: What is electric current?
“Electric current is a flow of tiny particles called electrons moving through a conductor — like water through a pipe.” If you rely on vague phrases like “it’s obvious”, that signals a gap to review.

Benefits

Why this method works

  • Deep understanding
    Explaining forces you to reconstruct ideas, not just recall words.
  • Find knowledge gaps
    Confusion becomes visible; you know what to study next.
  • Communicate clearly
    You’ll be able to teach others and perform better on exams.
